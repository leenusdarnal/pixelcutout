# PixelCutout model benchmark protocol

**Status:** Benchmark protocol and metadata validator are in place. AIM-500 is the primary local real-photo evaluation source under its official MIT release terms; the acquisition tool pins the mirror revision and carries the upstream MIT notice and required citation. The synthetic 16-case suite remains a separate smoke/regression set. Model scores and device measurements are still pending. See [the AIM-500 acquisition checklist](BENCHMARK_FIXTURE_ACQUISITION.md) and [the synthetic benchmark guide](SYNTHETIC_BENCHMARK.md).  
**Purpose:** Select the lightest model/runtime that meets PixelCutout's broad-subject and fine-edge quality requirements.  
**Product constraint:** All image pixels stay local. Benchmark images are test fixtures only and must never be included in the deployed site.

## 1. Benchmark set

### Project-owned synthetic smoke suite

PixelCutout now includes a separate 16-case procedural suite generated by `benchmarks/generate_synthetic.py`. It has exact 8-bit alpha ground truth and no external image inputs. Use it to validate the generation/scoring pipeline, exercise known edge structures, and detect regressions. See [SYNTHETIC_BENCHMARK.md](SYNTHETIC_BENCHMARK.md) for generation and case definitions.

This synthetic suite is **not** a substitute for the real-photo set below. Procedural illustrations do not establish performance on natural hair, fur, skin, reflective objects, or commercial photography. Keep synthetic results in a separate scorecard; do not pool them with AIM-500 or use them alone to choose the production model.


Use the same 16 images for every model candidate. Preserve a high-quality local original and create a standardised 2,048-pixel-long-edge evaluation copy for comparable inference timing. Keep original aspect ratio and do not sharpen, denoise, or otherwise alter subject edges.

| ID | Category | Required challenge | What a good cutout preserves |
|---|---|---|---|
| B01 | Person / hair | Straight dark hair against a dark or detailed background | Hair outline without background holes |
| B02 | Person / hair | Curly or coiled hair with flyaways and backlighting | Fine strands and soft hair edges |
| B03 | Person / clothing | Full-body pose with loose clothing, straps, or gaps | Limbs, fabric edges, and real openings |
| B04 | Pet / fur | Long-haired dog or cat with varied fur colours | Fur texture without a hard cutout edge |
| B05 | Pet / low contrast | Dark animal against a dark background | Complete subject silhouette |
| B06 | Pet / low contrast | White animal against a bright background | Ears, whiskers, and pale fur |
| B07 | Product | Small product on a clean white background with a cast shadow | Product shape without excessive shadow removal |
| B08 | Product / low contrast | Dark product against a dark or similarly coloured surface | Product boundary and controls |
| B09 | Product / translucent | Glass, clear bottle, or reflective object | Transparent/reflection detail where possible; record limitations |
| B10 | Everyday object | Irregular household object with handles, holes, or thin parts | Full object contour and negative spaces |
| B11 | Plant / leaves | Plant with many overlapping leaves and thin stems | Individual leaves and stem edges |
| B12 | Fine structure | Bicycle spokes, mesh chair, or wire basket | Thin lines and holes between them |
| B13 | Fine material | Lace, netting, or patterned fabric | Open weave and fine cut-through areas |
| B14 | Natural detail | Small branches, feathers, or similar delicate structure | Thin branches/feather edges |
| B15 | Difficult background | Subject and background share similar colours or texture | Subject completeness with low background leakage |
| B16 | Occlusion / busy scene | Multiple overlapping subjects or cluttered surroundings | Intended foreground objects without background fragments |

Use AIM-500 for the first natural-photo comparison under the official release agreement: the Dataset is stated to be MIT-licensed, the agreement says the photos came from websites with free-use licences, and photo copyright remains with original owners. The local acquisition script carries the upstream MIT notice (`Copyright (c) 2021 Jizhizi_Li`) and required IJCAI 2021 citation. Keep the raw photos/mattes local and do not redistribute them; the mirror does not supply original per-photo source URLs/licence URLs. For any additional dataset or any external redistribution/independent reuse of an AIM-500 photograph, verify the image owner and its own licence/attribution terms. Keep source images and ground-truth mattes separate from model assets and the application bundle.

## 2. Keep benchmark assets out of the application

Store local test images under `benchmarks/images/`, which must be git-ignored. Do not place them under `static/`, `public/`, or any Vite-imported source directory. Commit only this protocol and a metadata manifest (ID, filename, source, creator, licence, SHA-256, dimensions). Do not commit the test image binaries or copy them to the deployed site.

Each local image should have a SHA-256 digest recorded in the manifest so that model comparisons use identical inputs. Keep a separate notes column for known image-specific difficulty or ground-truth availability.

## 3. Quality scoring

Score each result visually from 1 (unusable) to 5 (excellent) in these dimensions:

| Dimension | Weight | Review focus |
|---|---:|---|
| Subject preservation | 30% | No missing parts, holes, or accidental transparency inside the subject |
| Fine-edge retention | 30% | Hair, fur, leaves, threads, spokes, and delicate structure remain convincing |
| Background removal | 20% | Background leakage and disconnected remnants are minimal |
| Halo / edge cleanliness | 10% | No obvious bright/dark fringe, jagged edge, or colour contamination |
| Consistency / artifacts | 10% | No unexpected blobs, blur, or unstable output across the image |

For images with reliable ground-truth masks, also record mask IoU/F1 and boundary F-score at a fixed pixel tolerance. These metrics supplement, but do not replace, visual review of alpha edges and translucent material. Do not invent ground truth for images that do not have it.

A model candidate should not be selected solely because it is small or fast. Flag any critical failure where an important part of the subject disappears, the background remains as a large connected region, or the mask damages important fine structures.

## 4. Runtime and performance protocol

For each candidate, record:

- Exact model repository, revision/commit, licence, runtime version, and model file names.
- Actual model download bytes, runtime/WASM bytes, and total first-use transfer bytes separately.
- Cold start including model loading, then median of three warm inference runs.
- Peak memory or the best available device memory indicator.
- WebGPU and WASM results separately; record unsupported or failed combinations rather than silently excluding them.
- Device, browser/version, operating system, image ID, dimensions, and whether assets were already cached.

Use the same device, browser version, test image, cache state, and input dimensions when comparing candidates. Run at least one desktop-class and one mid-range Android device before choosing the production default. Do not claim first-load performance based only on Vite emitted asset sizes; use a browser network waterfall. For the primary soft-alpha scorecard, run `npm run benchmark:validate -- --require-alpha-complete` before scoring; binary segmentation cases, if retained as an extension, must be reported separately.

## 6. Selection rule

1. Eliminate candidates with incompatible or unclear licences before benchmarking.
2. Reject candidates that produce critical failures in common subject groups or consistently damage fine edges.
3. Among candidates that meet the quality bar, choose the lightest and most compatible runtime.
4. Keep one production model/runtime path unless measured evidence supports a second path.
5. Record the final decision and trade-offs in this file and update `docs/PROJECT_TRACKER.md`.

**Current conclusion:** no replacement model has been selected. MODNet remains the production baseline. A practical general-subject candidate has been identified for controlled evaluation: studioludens/birefnet-lite-512 (MIT, 94 MB fp16 / 183 MB fp32 as stated by its model card). The candidate's 512×512 fixed input may trade detail for browser memory, and the model card's performance and quality claims have not been independently reproduced. The existing MODNet q4f16 asset is 11.8 MB; the Lite candidate is substantially larger, so do not switch until the same image set has been scored on desktop and mid-range Android. See MODEL_PROVENANCE.md for candidate source links and the decision gate. The Hugging Face Hub reported `fa2fa546052fba4c08921230a26cc69a333fca12` as the current repository revision when checked on 2026-11-22; PixelCutout does not yet pin that revision at runtime because the installed Transformers.js 4.3.2 has an upstream revision-propagation defect. See [`MODEL_PROVENANCE.md`](MODEL_PROVENANCE.md). The 16-case set must be populated and candidates measured before a model decision.


## 7. Candidate research notes (2026-11-22)

The following candidate is a research lead, not a tested result:

- Repository: https://huggingface.co/studioludens/birefnet-lite-512
- Source architecture and weights: https://huggingface.co/ZhengPeng7/BiRefNet_lite, whose upstream repository declares MIT.
- Candidate repository's own licence file: MIT. Observed repository commit at the time of review: https://huggingface.co/studioludens/birefnet-lite-512/commit/962e67969ec6acec9c19fab76acee4f5e22e990c.
- Candidate model card reports 94 MB fp16 and 183 MB fp32 ONNX weights at fixed 512×512 input.
- Model card documents an AutoModel + AutoProcessor Transformers.js flow. It says output logits need sigmoid, then resizing to the original image dimensions.
- The author reports that the 1024×1024 ONNX variant exceeded browser memory in their tests and that the 512×512 export was made to reduce peak memory. Those are upstream claims, not PixelCutout test results.

Compared with the existing MODNet q4f16 file (11.8 MB) the fp16 BiRefNet Lite candidate is about eight times larger. It may offer better coverage beyond portraits, but the 512×512 input may soften fine details when expanded to large output dimensions. Both quality and runtime cost must be measured before changing the production worker.

Do not benchmark the candidate using a different input set, different resizing, or different cache state from MODNet. Record actual bytes transferred, cold-start time, warm inference median, peak memory or an available proxy, and separate WebGPU/WASM success on both desktop and mid-range Android. Do not mark the benchmark complete based on model-card claims.
